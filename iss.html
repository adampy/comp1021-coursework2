International Space Station

The International Space Station (ISS) is a space station in low Earth orbit, and is one of two currently operational space stations in orbit. The construction of the ISS started in 1998 and it was assembled in orbit by 42 assembly flights.[1]

The ISS has over 1.5 million lines of code on the U.S. segment, and keeps tabs on 400,000 different data points. These data points are critical to the health of the astronauts living in the station, who don't see their families for prolonged periods of times.

Orbital Debris

Orbital debris (space debris) is a huge problem that most people underestimate. The ISS has an orbital velocity of 7.6km/s[1] so any collision could be catastrophic.

How are orbital debris avoided?

To avoid debris, the ISS has to increase and decrease its altitude. It does this once a year on average[3], but as more and more satellites and spacecraft are launched there will be more orbital debris present in low-Earth orbit.

Future of the ISS

The ISS is becoming more and more expensive to run as it gets older and requires more maintenance, and the future of the ISS is set to end in 2031. NASA plans to deorbit the station into the Pacific Ocean and will pay commercial companies in exchange for time on their crafts. NASA scientists will use the time bought on commerically funded stations to carry out experiments, as this will be deemed more cost effective.[4]
