Apollo 11

The first spaceflight that landed humans on the Lunar surface was Apollo 11. Landing on July 21st 1969, the Saturn V rocket carried three astronauts whose names the world would never forget:

Neil A. Armstrong

Commander

Micheal Collins

Command Module Pilot

Edwin "Buzz" E. Aldrin Jr.

Lunar Module Pilot

About Apollo 11

Apollo 11 was a mission to the Moon that lasted 8 days and 3 hours in total, and brought back 21.6kg[1] of Moon samples. At this point in time, scientists had no idea whether or not there was life on the Moon, and these samples were the foundations of proving that there was no life on the Moon. Once the three astronauts returned to Earth, they were placed in a mobile quarantine for a total of 21 days ! This was to ensure they brought no foreign life forms from the Moon back to Earth.

Why explore Space?

The motivation behind space exploration, including the Apollo prorgams, was further enhanced by the Cold War[2]. The Cold War was not actually a war, but was a period in recent history in which there was heightened tensions between the US (United States) and the USSR (now known as Russia). There was a space race between the two superpowers, with each one trying to reach the Lunar surface before the other. In the end the US won, but it was fair to say that both sides had used eachother's newly developed technology to get their - in a sense, it was a joint effort!
